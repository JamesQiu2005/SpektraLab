# DECISIONS-FOR-OWNER.md: eight words for you to choose

Written 2026-10-22. In each of these eight cases, people who speak Japanese well would choose
differently. Each one starts with my recommendation, then gives what the other choice would cost. You do not need to read Japanese to decide.
The translation is going ahead with the recommended word, so if you agree, nothing more needs to happen.
To change one, say "D3: the other one" and the translator swaps the word everywhere.

Your rule was: kanji first, katakana as little as possible, English only where needed. Where I
recommend katakana below, it is because the kanji form would sound old, odd or unclear to a Japanese
photographer. In every other case I chose kanji.

---

**D1. "Print" (the stage between develop and scan): 焼き付け (yakitsuke), recommended, over プリント (purinto).**
焼き付け is the darkroom's own word, kanji, and it means "burning the image onto paper". Japanese
photo labs advertised the whole service as 現像・焼付・引伸 (develop, print, enlarge). Your website's
tagline already uses the same verb: 写真を、焼く ("burn the photograph"). *Cost:* the website says
プリント everywhere else, and プリント is what most younger users say. 焼き付け is understood by everyone
and reads as more craft-like, which suits a film app. If you agree, the site's body text should say 焼き付け too.

**D2. "Library": ライブラリ (raiburari, katakana), recommended, over a kanji word such as 一覧 (ichiran, "list").**
Apple's own Photos app calls its tab ライブラリ. A user who looks for "the library" looks for that word. There is no kanji word in
common use for it: 一覧 means "a list" and 写真庫 ("photo storehouse") would be made up. *Cost:* one
more katakana word, and a frequent one.

**D3. "Highlights / Shadows": 明部 / 暗部 (meibu / anbu, kanji), recommended, over ハイライト / シャドウ (katakana).**
明部 and 暗部 are the photographic words for "the bright part" and "the dark part", and are used in
exposure and printing books (明部・中間部・暗部). They are short (two characters), which helps the
tiles. *Cost:* Apple Photos and Lightroom both say ハイライト / シャドウ, so a user coming from those
apps sees a different word for the same slider. They will still understand it at once. This choice
also decides "protect highlights", "highlight placement" and the colour wheels' zones.

**D4. "Film Edge": フィルムの縁 (firumu no fuchi, "the film's border"), recommended, over フィルムエッジ (firumu ejji, the website's word).**
縁 is the ordinary word for an edge or border, and it brings the feature's other words with it:
縁の印字 ("printing on the border") instead of エッジプリント, and 縁かぶり ("border fog"). *Cost:* the
website uses フィルムエッジ as a section title. The film community knows エッジプリント as a term, so
the kanji version is plainer and less "branded". If you keep the katakana, both words change back:
フィルムエッジ and エッジプリント.

**D5. "Negative / Positive / Slide": ネガ / ポジ / リバーサル (katakana), recommended, over the kanji 陰画 / 陽画 (inga / yōga).**
This is the one place where the kanji is the wrong choice. 陰画 and 陽画 are real words, but they
are old: they appear in old textbooks and dictionaries, and no photographer says them today. Every
Japanese film shooter says ネガ, ポジ and リバーサル. Using the kanji would make the app sound like a
1950s manual. *Cost:* three katakana words.

**D6. "Latitude": 寛容度 (kan'yōdo, "tolerance", kanji), recommended, over ラチチュード (rachichūdo, the website's word).**
寛容度 (in full 露光寛容度, "exposure tolerance") is the textbook term, and photographers recognise it.
In everyday speech they say ラチチュード ("this film has wide latitude"). *Cost:* it is a little
formal, and the website's latitude card says ラチチュード. The Chinese word 宽容度 looks similar but is
written differently: the app uses the Japanese form.

**D7. "Half-frame pair": ハーフ判の2コマ組 ("a two-frame set in half format"), with 2コマ組 as the short label, recommended, over ペア (pea, "pair", the website's word).**
Japanese has no standard term for this feature. 2コマ組 is made from ordinary parts, on the pattern of
2枚組 ("a set of two", as in a two-disc album), and a Japanese reader understands it immediately.
It is a construction, not a fixed term. *Cost:* the website says ハーフサイズのペア. ペア is
simpler, but it is katakana and less precise. コマ (frame) is itself written in katakana by convention,
even though it is a native Japanese word.

**D8. "Original" (the photo before editing, and its file): 元画像 / 元ファイル (moto-gazō / moto-fairu, "the source image / file"), recommended, over オリジナル (orijinaru).**
Apple writes オリジナル in Photos ("compare with the original") and in the iCloud setting "download and
keep originals". The app uses "original" in many places: the capsule, missing-file messages, Keep
Originals, storage. 元画像 and 元ファイル are plain and precise, and they keep "the picture" and "the
file" apart, as your English keys already do. *Cost:* users who know Apple's word see a different one.
It is not a system button, so nothing breaks.

## Decided without asking, for the record

These were clear enough to decide, and each is explained in `GLOSSARY.md`:
- Date Back → 日付写し込み ("date imprinting", the word Japanese camera menus use), not デートバック.
- Export recipe → 書き出し設定 ("export setting"), not レシピ: in Japan "recipe" means a Fujifilm-style colour look, which would mislead.
- Full size → 原寸 ("original size"), never フルサイズ, which in Japanese means a 35 mm full-frame sensor.
- Log → 動作記録 ("record of how it runs"), not ログ.
- Film and paper names stay exactly as in English (Kodak Portra 400…), the catalogue's own.
- The website's light-table page uses 画幅 for film format. That is a Chinese photographic word, and the page should say 判型 instead.
